# CeCe chick: mechanism and packaging experiment

The original `chick_concept.scad` is preserved. `chick_packaging.scad` is a
separate, larger two-AA candidate generated by `chick_sim.py`. It is deliberately
a simple packaging envelope, not a final chick shell or printable assembly.

## See it move

Run `./start_chick_sim.ps1` for the **fixed bench** joint experiment.
Run `./start_chick_sim.ps1 -Mode walk` for the **unsupported floor** experiment.
Run `./start_chick_sim.ps1 -Mode check` to regenerate reports and preview.

In the viewer: **P** pauses/resumes, **R** resets. Mouse buttons rotate/pan/zoom.
Under Rendering, disable geom group **1** to hide the shell or **2** to hide
the internal purchased-component envelopes. The default shell is translucent.
Bench mode has a fixed torso; it demonstrates joint travel, not walking.
Free-floor mode has a six-degree-of-freedom base, gravity and contact. Body
translation is never scripted. A reset returns to the initial pose.

The launcher currently reuses the installed Python environment in the sibling
`coffee-robot` folder. Alternatively install `requirements-sim.txt` in a local
`.venv`; the launcher prefers that. No additional packages were installed.

## Results, 26 October 2026

- One hip joint, one assumed N20 output actuator. Right leg fixed to torso;
  left leg rotates relative to it. This is one explicit interpretation of the
  previously undefined one-motor mechanism, not an existing engineered linkage.
- Command: sinusoidal 14 degree amplitude at 1.5 Hz, one-second startup ramp.
- Assumed output limit: 0.020 N m; no-load speed 10 rad/s, linear torque-speed
  reduction. PD feedback assumes angle sensing that has not been selected.
  This is not an electrical motor, gearbox-loss, current or battery simulation.
- Six-second bench trial: about -13.79 to +13.79 degrees, RMS tracking error
  1.10 degrees after startup. This establishes numerical motion, not real motor
  suitability or a completed shaft, bearing, coupling or attachment design.
- Six-second free-floor trials at friction 0.4, 0.7 and 1.0 all tip over and put
  the body on the floor. **Walking fails for this candidate.** Displacement
  caused by falling is not counted as walking.

## Internal components

Candidate outer torso: **64 x 48 x 64 mm**, 2 mm nominal wall. Overall height
approximately **142 mm**, instead of the original 100 mm appearance concept.
Estimated mass **116.2 g**, including **48 g** for two AA cells; every mass is
an assumption requiring measurement. It exceeds the original 80 g target.

The battery holder occupies the left of the body; the SuperMini, H-bridge,
regulator and audio amplifier occupy the right. The speaker and microphone
occupy the head. Neither audio chamber nor acoustic isolation is engineered.
The 60 x 33 x 18 mm holder assumption from the earlier duck work is superseded
here by a vertically oriented **18 x 34 x 55 mm** envelope. Measure the actual
holder, terminals and wire exit before using it.

Static axis-aligned component boxes have no unintended overlaps. Body boxes
fit the rectangular inner cavity, and head-box corners fit the inner ellipsoid.
AA cells intentionally sit inside their holder. This is a **static envelope
check**, not a general collision-proof or manufacturability claim. Mounted
hardware, headers, cables, USB access, cooling, antenna clearance, shell screws,
moving clearances and detailed retaining geometry remain unfinished.

Two AA cells supply approximately 3 V nominal in series. A suitable power
converter, driver and power budget must be selected before wiring. Motor
voltage, startup current and gearbox ratio are still unspecified.

## Next mechanical decision

Do not fabricate this candidate as a working walker. Its heavy, high battery
and one-joint architecture need a balance/mechanism redesign. Options include
lower horizontal cells with a wider body, revised rocking feet and ballast
distribution, or an explicit crank/weight-shift linkage. Re-run free-floor
trials after each change and verify the actual motor under load.
